## WHAT'S IN THE BOX {#whats-in-the-box}

The Meet 2 is a small camera. In the box I found:

- A multilingual user manual
- A USB-C to USB-C cable, plus a USB-A to USB-C adapter
- A metal privacy cover, which turned out to be magnetic and easy to put on and take off
- A monitor mount with more than one adjustment
- The camera itself, with a 1/4-20 thread on the body

That 1/4-20 mount is what later let me move it from the monitor to an overhead arm.

## SPECS AND THE MONITOR MOUNT {#specs}

On paper, the Meet 2 has a 1/2-inch sensor, an f/1.8 lens, and 4K at 30 fps or 1080p at 60 fps. Autofocus is phase detection, and OBSBOT says it focuses down to 10 cm. Around the body there is little else to see: a built-in microphone (two pinhole mics, in practice), USB-C on the back, and that magnetic cover.

One more spec I came back to later: the cable is USB-C, but the data runs at USB 2.0.

I used the included monitor mount for the front-facing tests, then used the 1/4-20 thread for a downward-facing overhead test.

The video also mentions the Meet SE as a lower-cost option if you do not need 4K. As I described it on camera, the SE drops to 1080p and a smaller sensor, but it can run up to 100 frames per second. I did not test the SE for this review.

## FIRST POWER-UP, FIRMWARE, AND THE M1 QUESTION {#m1}

The workbench computer is an older Apple M1 Mac mini. It is not the fastest Mac in the house, and that was the point. The question was not only whether the camera looked good, but whether an M1 would run two cameras in OBS Studio without wheezing.

As soon as I plugged the Meet 2 in, OBSBOT Center offered a firmware update. I applied it before the rest of the tests. The camera came up, and the image was not upside down. OBSBOT Center wants macOS 11 or later. Most of that requirement is for beauty and background effects at 4K, which I do not use. An M1 is the minimum, and that is what I had on the bench: an M1 with 16 GB of RAM.

## THE VOX SE MIC, AND WHAT THE MEET 2 DOES NOT DO {#vox-se}

Parts of the video use an OBSBOT VOX SE microphone. That mic was connected through my Tiny 3. The Meet 2 does not connect to the VOX SE, so you need a Tiny in the setup if you want that microphone. I used it for convenience, and because I later combined the Meet 2 with the Tiny 3 for a two-camera desktop setup. It is not a Meet 2 feature.

I recorded most of the video with the Meet 2 image, and I used its built-in mics in several sections so you can hear them. Some B-roll uses a different camera.

## 4K RECORDING AND FACE-CAMERA VIEWS {#face-cam}

On the bench, the Meet 2 kept up with 1080p at 60 fps for recording. The YouTube version is 24 fps either way. I also recorded a 4K30 check in OBSBOT Center's recording option, using the Meet 2 microphones, on that same M1 Mac, warts and all, so you can judge the clip in the video.

**VERIFY:** Right after that 4K question, I said that with both cameras live in OBS, the M1 held a steady 60 frames per second and CPU sat around 9 to 10%. Confirm whether that figure belongs to the 4K check, the later two-camera OBS setup, or both.

The face-camera lighting was not a proper key-light setup. I had workbench spotlights, left the background alone, and the image still looked good. There is a background blur option; I would probably add a little.

Fields of view in OBSBOT Center are wide, medium, and narrow. Auto framing will track me as long as I stay on the sensor. It is not a mechanical gimbal. To use that framing I had to set the video capture to 1080p, which leaves you four quadrants of the 4K sensor to move around in. If I step well off to the side, the camera cannot follow.

For bench work I wanted a fixed frame, so I turned auto framing off.

## ZOOM GESTURE, BUILT-IN MICS, AND AUTOFOCUS {#features}

The Meet 2 does not include PTZ hand gestures, but it does have a zoom gesture: an L shape next to your forehead zooms in, and the same gesture zooms back out. That is useful when you want a quick punch-in and do not want to hunt for a keyboard, mouse, or remote.

The two pinhole mics have settings in OBSBOT Center under audio: microphone status during sleep, plus noise reduction off, weak, medium, and strong. In the video I walked through those modes with some ambient sound in the room. For the later overhead audio check, I was about a foot away, then about six inches, with auto gain off and medium noise reduction on.

The autofocus test used a Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 box, then my face, then a closer hold. I measured about 4 inches from the lens at the close point, which is right around OBSBOT's 10 cm figure. Focus from the box to my face was fast. One retry had a short hiccup, which is typical of cameras like this. That result is what made me want to try it overhead.

## OVERHEAD ON THE ARM {#overhead}

I moved the Meet 2 to a downward position over the bench, with the Tiny 3 still on face-camera duty. To mount it I used a 22-inch adjustable magic arm with a 1/4-20 thread and a clamp. **VERIFY:** auto-captions heard the brand as “Jabutu.” Confirm the actual arm before this line is final.

The arm let me level the camera and keep the USB-C cable from dropping into the shot. An OBSBOT remote let me switch between the two cameras while recording and make adjustments on the Meet 2. Because I was not zoomed all the way out, I still had some leftover 4K sensor area, so I could nudge the frame up and down and zoom in on a case texture or a small tool.

The 4x zoom is digital. It is a crop, not a lens zoom, and not in-camera optics. For overhead work it still looked good. Autofocus could blur if I moved an object too fast, then it locked. I used an Innovate for Us lithium precision screwdriver kit as a close-up subject: opening the case, showing the contents, and punching in on a small brush.

I did not go into this test expecting it to work this well. On the bench, the Meet 2 is a strong overhead option for detail work, drawing, or electronics. You could also run two Meet 2 cameras, one for a face view and one overhead, if you do not want two PTZ units.

## TWO CAMERAS IN OBS STUDIO {#obs}

I then put the Meet 2 back on the monitor and added it in OBS Studio next to the Tiny 3. Both devices were recognized by name, and it was straightforward to set up a head shot and a second view. When I enabled the Meet 2 as an audio source, the mics came in pretty hot, so I lowered the fader and renamed the source. Noise reduction and other audio tweaks stay in OBSBOT Center.

I also tried holding the Meet 2 in my hand, the way you might for a quick vlog clip. The camera is small enough for that, and small enough to throw in a bag with a MacBook Air or MacBook Pro. The image is a lot better than the camera built into my Mac.

I ran the two-camera setup for about an hour, and it never dropped out.

## VERDICT {#verdict}

Did the Meet 2 do the job on this bench? I believe it did. I still need to adjust the workbench lighting if I want the best image, but I also tested it in some of the worst lighting I had available, and it held up well.

The reason to skip it is if you want something oversized or bulky. If you want something small that can live on a monitor, move to an overhead arm, or ride along with a laptop, this one did those jobs in my tests.

I did not give the Meet 2 a numeric score in the video, so I am not inventing one here.

## CONCLUSION {#conclusion}

The Meet 2 is a small 4K webcam with phase-detection autofocus, a magnetic cover, and a 1/4-20 thread that makes it easy to move from a monitor mount to an arm. On an M1 Mac mini it worked as a face camera and, more surprisingly to me, as an overhead camera for close-up bench work. Auto framing is a 1080p crop of the 4K sensor, not a gimbal. The zoom gesture is a real convenience. The built-in mics are usable, though they ran hot in OBS and the VOX SE will not attach through this camera.

If you already own a Tiny 3, the Meet 2 is a sensible second camera. If you do not, two Meet 2 units would cover a simple face-plus-overhead setup.
