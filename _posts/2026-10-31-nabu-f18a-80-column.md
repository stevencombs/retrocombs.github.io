What if your NABU could do 80 columns? I replaced its TMS9918ANL video chip with an F18A to find out. No, not that kind of F18A. This F18A is an FPGA board that adds 80-column mode to the NABU personal computer, and it keeps 40-column mode, too.

This post is the text companion to my video. It covers what the F18A is, why I wanted it, the tools you need, a step-by-step install (including the two pins that got in my way), and my tests: the NABU network, 40- and 80-column Cloud CP/M, an MSX ROM, a composite-versus-VGA comparison, and WordStar. Let's dig in!

## WHAT IS THE F18A? {#what-is-the-f18a}

The F18A is a pin-compatible replacement for the TMS9918A family of video display processors (VDPs), used in many retro computers and game consoles from the early-to-mid 1980s. The NABU uses that chip, as do the TI-99/4A and many MSX computers. The F18A's main goal is pixel-perfect video output you can use directly with a computer display.

There are two versions. [The F18A introduction page](https://dnotq.io/f18a/intro.html) has the details.

- **Original F18A:** the one I installed. It has VGA out.
- **F18A Mark II:** HDMI out instead of VGA, and a smaller form factor. You'll see why that smaller size matters during my install.

The F18A also offers more graphics modes. I don't cover those here.

## WHY 80-COLUMN MODE? {#why-80-column}

Cloud CP/M is available for the NABU in two versions: a 40-column version, and now an 80-column version that supports the F18A. If you've worked in CP/M or MS-DOS, you know 80 columns gives you more breathing room in text-based software.

With the 40-column version, you can get "sort of" 80 columns by pressing the page right and page left keys on the NABU keyboard. That scrolls the screen left and right, so you're always trying to see what's off to one side.

## TOOLS YOU NEED {#tools}

- **A static-free work area.** Some of you will want an anti-static wrist strap. I don't use one. Tell me in the comments below why I should.
- **A small flathead screwdriver** to slowly pry the old chip out of its socket, or a chip puller
- **A Phillips screwdriver.** A precision screwdriver set is handy.

## STEP 1: INSTALL THE BACKPLATE {#backplate}

I was feeling positive about this project, so I installed the backplate first. The F18A comes with a 3D-printed PLA backplate, which is a nice touch. You can do this step at the beginning or the end. I'm a glass-half-full kind of guy.

1. Remove one of the blank plates from the back of the NABU. There are several. Pick a location.
2. Put the PLA backplate in its place.
3. Reuse the NABU's original screws. They bite nicely into the PLA.

## STEP 2: REMOVE THE TMS CHIP {#remove-tms}

The TMS9918ANL video display chip sits toward the back of the main board, left of center. Use a chip puller, or do what I did: use a very small flathead screwdriver to pry the chip out slowly and gently. Be very careful if you use that method.

## STEP 3: INSTALL THE F18A {#install-f18a}

When I placed the F18A, two connector pins got in the way. To make room, I bent them back about 15 degrees toward the back of the case. I also relocated the header on one of the pins. After that, the F18A went into the socket easily. This is where the Mark II's smaller size would have helped.

## STEP 4: CONNECT THE CABLE AND A MONITOR {#cable-and-monitor}

1. Connect the cable to the F18A. Luckily, mine had an orientation warning printed right on it, so I knew which way was up.
2. Route the VGA port on the other end of the cable to the backplate. It didn't look half bad. I'm not sure I would have picked purple. I may reprint it in black or silver.
3. Leave the top off the NABU until you've tested it.
4. Plug the VGA port into a monitor. I used my handy-dandy little Wimaxit monitor. There's a [YouTube Short](https://youtube.com/shorts/i0osPNK4a2M) if you want to learn more about it.

## TESTING THE F18A {#testing}

Time to fire it up:

- NABU welcome screen: ✅ Check
- NABU network, 40-column original graphics: ✅ Check. The F18A emulates the original TMS chip.
- 40-column Cloud CP/M: ✅ Check
- NABU network: ✅ Check
- MSX ROM (Galaga): ✅ Check

Everything that worked with the original TMS chip still works, and the picture is cleaner.

## COMPOSITE VS. VGA {#composite-vs-vga}

Before the F18A, I captured video from the NABU's RCA (composite) jack. With VGA, the display is much crisper, and the colors look sharper. I'm fortunate to have a [ClonerAlliance Box Pro](https://amzn.to/4qAOn3A), which has both composite and VGA inputs, so the video shows the same screen captured both ways. There's a marked improvement.

## 80-COLUMN MODE AND WORDSTAR {#80-column-test}

This is what I wanted the F18A for. I booted Cloud CP/M in its 80-column mode for the F18A, and there it was: 80 columns. Check.

Then I tried WordStar in 80 columns versus 40. It's a much better experience. If I wanted to write my next blog post in WordStar on the NABU, I could. Hmm, that's an idea.

With everything working, I put the cover back on the NABU and set it back on the VEVOR rack.

## CONCLUSION {#conclusion}

I'm very pleased with the F18A. It did everything it promised, it was a great purchase, and I recommend it for anyone with a NABU. If you can find the Mark II, you'll get HDMI out and avoid the pin interference I ran into.
